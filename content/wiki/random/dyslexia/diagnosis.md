There are a few specific "low-cost" workarounds in the Las Vegas area.

1. Vocational Rehabilitation (The Employment Path)\
If the dyslexia is impacting the adult's ability to get a job, keep a job, or advance in their career, Bureau of Vocational Rehabilitation (BVR) is your best resource.
How it works: They are a state agency that helps people with disabilities find or maintain employment. If they suspect a learning disability is a barrier to work, they will often pay for the full diagnostic evaluation as part of their intake process.
Cost: Free to those who qualify.
Action: Contact the Southern Nevada office at (702) 486-5230 or visit their office at 3405 S. Maryland Pkwy. Tell them you believe you have an undiagnosed learning disability that is interfering with your work.
2. UNLV PRACTICE (The Clinical Path)\
The Psychological Assessment and Testing Clinic (PATC) at UNLV is the most reliable low-cost clinical option for adults in Clark County.
Pricing: They use a sliding fee scale based on your household income. While a private psychologist might charge $2,500, UNLV typically ranges between $300 and $1,000 for a full battery of tests.
Waitlist: Because they are low-cost, their waitlist can be long (often several months). It is best to get on the list as soon as possible.
Contact: Call (702) 895-1532 or email PATC@unlv.edu.
3. Dyslexia Resource Center (The Specialized Path)\
There is a local organization called the Dyslexia Resource Center (Dyslexia 1in5) that specifically focuses on this.
Services: They offer comprehensive dyslexia testing for both children and adults.
Cost: Their listed fee for a comprehensive evaluation is approximately $500. This is significantly lower than standard neuropsychological exams.
Contact: You can reach them through their website at dyslexia1n5.com.
4. Self-Screening (The Preliminary Path)\
Before spending money, the International Dyslexia Association (IDA) offers a "Self-Assessment for Adults." While this is not a legal diagnosis, it can provide data to bring to a clinic to justify a more formal (and expensive) evaluation.
The IDA 2026 Annual Conference is actually scheduled to be in Las Vegas, which may result in more local workshops or free screening events early next year
